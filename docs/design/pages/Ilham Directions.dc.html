Shipping — Apparatus, both grounds

The system: Apparatus, light and dark

Apparatus is the shipped direction. 2a is its dark calibration — recalculated ink, a desaturated accent, Arabic held at Regular rather than bumped heavier — and 1c below is the light default. Both now carry the three finalisation changes: the 12px label floor with 15px rail body (text ratio 4.3:1), a named chain density that fits ten narrators on one screen, and Pine removed so the system is back to a single accent. 1a and 1b stay on the board as the rejected alternatives.

2A Dark ground · Arabic first

Apparatus — dark

Same skeleton as 1c: 44px Arabic as the object on the table, a 210px English rail at 12px, one accent, no serif English, ratio 4.3:1. Everything that changes here changes for an optical reason, not a stylistic one.

What the evidence says, and where it disagrees
Ground is not black

Pure black maximises the luminance delta that triggers halation and eye strain, so accessibility guidance points to a near-black charcoal instead — commonly #121212 to #1E293B on non-OLED screens. Ilham uses #16181A. colorcontrast.org, 2026

Ink is not white

Pure white on dark produces the strongest halation and ghosting, described as actively painful for readers with astigmatism; the standing advice is a softened white and a body target at or above 7:1. #E4E2DC measures 13.7:1 on the ground and 12.5:1 on the panel surface. DubBot

Accents desaturate

Saturated hues both fail contrast and vibrate on dark surfaces; guidance is roughly 20 points less saturation than the light-mode value. The single accent moves #2437C4 → #93A8F5. atmos.style

Weight — sources conflict

One camp says bump body weight to 450–500 because halation thins light strokes (fontfyi); another says drop to ~350 because light text already reads bolder (kosmoweb). My read, not a sourced finding: the thinning argument applies at body size, and Ilham's Arabic is at 44px, where bloom dominates. So Arabic stays Regular; only the 11px rail labels step up to 600.

Worth stating plainly, because it is the strongest evidence on this page and it cuts against dark mode: reading research reports a "positive polarity advantage" — black-on-white measures more legible than white-on-black, attributed to the brighter illumination of positive-polarity displays (literature review, Information Design Journal, 2023). Those studies are on Latin text; I found no equivalent controlled study on vocalised Naskh, so this variant should ship as a user-chosen alternative to 1c, not as the default.

Type — what changes from 1c
Arabic 44 / 1.95

Same 44px, same Scheherazade New Regular, line-height 1.9 → 1.95. Vowel marks bloom on dark, and the extra 0.05 buys the clearance back without touching the size.

English 16 → 17

Body English up one pixel and tracking +0.01em, the standard optical-scaling correction for small text on dark. The 12px label floor holds, at weight 600.

Ratio 4.3:1 unchanged

The whole ladder is the same, so a screen can switch ground without reflowing. Only colour, the weight of the 12px labels, and two line-height values differ.

Implementation rules — both grounds

Nine rules the research settled

These apply to Apparatus light and dark alike and are not stylistic preferences — each one traces to a source in Ilham Evidence. Where the reasoning is mine rather than a source's, the report says so.

01 · Bidi isolation

Every Arabic string carries an explicit dir; database-sourced strings use dir="auto" or <bdi>. HTML markup, not the CSS property — it works without CSS, and the attribute isolates as well as directs. The rail's Latin transmission word inside an Arabic name is the case that breaks without it.

02 · No tracking on Arabic

Letter-spacing is zero on every Arabic element, at every size. Arabic is a joined script; tracking pulls the joins apart. The +0.12em tracking in this system belongs to 12px Latin labels only.

03 · No italic, no synthetic bold

Arabic is never slanted and never emphasised by weight — only Scheherazade New's Regular has a truly drawn Bold, so anything else risks a synthesised face. Emphasis on Arabic is a 2px underline in the accent. Italics are for English glosses.

04 · Ragged, never justified

Arabic is right-aligned and ragged everywhere. Inter-word justification produces rivers and uneven colour, letter-level adjustment carries Arabic-specific problems, and there is no hyphenation to fall back on.

05 · Numerals, on the record

Machine values use Western digits, because they sit inside an English apparatus rather than inside Arabic prose. Stated as a decision, not a default: the i18n libraries treat Arabic-Indic digits as the set for Arabic-language text, and web products diverge from that widely.

06 · Contrast floors

Light ground: 4.5:1 body, 3:1 for UI and hairlines; every figure quoted against the background it actually sits on. Dark ground: 7:1 for body, because the AAA threshold is a real readability gain once halation is in play — and measured on the panel surface, not just the ground, since that is where the rail text sits. Shipped: ink 12.5:1, apparatus ink 8.0:1, accent 7.1:1, hairlines 3.02:1 on ground.

07 · Focus, outline only

A 2px solid outline at 2px offset, on :focus-visible, with a forced-colors fallback. Never box-shadow — it disappears in Windows High Contrast Mode. 2px is the published minimum, not the old 1px.

08 · Colour, fenced

One accent, blue, meaning position only. No green, no gold, no red anywhere. Grade and weight are never coloured. Near-black and green never appear together. If the screen works in greyscale, it works.

09 · QA, off the desk

Two checks no contrast tool performs: read the dark ground in a dim room on an OLED panel at low brightness, and proof fully vowelled Arabic at final size, where stacked marks fail first. Also verify mark placement in the shipping font — kasra with shadda moves.

Open — needs the teacher

The pairing test. Build the chain viewer twice, the second on IBM Plex Sans with IBM Plex Sans Arabic, and choose in front of the class. The sources favour one drawn family; Apparatus mixes deliberately. That trade is not mine to settle.

Ilham — design system proposals

Three directions for a bilingual hadith instrument

Each column below is rendered in its own system: its own ground, type, palette and density rules, with the same dense specimen — one hadith, a six-narrator chain, two grades per narrator, machine values — so the three can be compared on the hardest screen rather than on a swatch strip. Reference them in chat as 1a, 1b, 1c.

1A Light ground · warm paper

Codex

The corpus is a printed book and the study layer is a set of notes in its margins. Warm paper, one text column, hairline rules, no boxes around anything that isn't interactive. Beginners are reassured by something that looks like a well-set edition rather than a database console.

Why light: sessions run an hour in daylit libraries and classrooms, and Naskh at reading size has fine joins that thicken and blur on a dark ground. Paper-warm rather than white keeps a page of black Arabic from vibrating.

1B Dark ground · instrument

Register

Not a book — an instrument for reading 139,629 links. The whole interface is a ruled grid, values are aligned to the character cell, and Arabic sits in the one place the grid gives way. Made for the student who is comparing four chains at once and the teacher watching a class list.

1C Light ground · Arabic first

Apparatus — light

The other two directions treat Arabic as content inside an English product. This one inverts it: the Arabic is the object on the table, set very large, and the entire English interface shrinks to a quiet apparatus around it — labels at 12px, one accent, no serifs anywhere.

Why light: when Arabic is set at 44px it becomes the largest mass on the screen, and a large dark mass on a dark ground turns the page into a poster. Near-white paper keeps the text a text. The ground is cool rather than warm so the Arabic reads as a specimen under glass, not as a facsimile of a book.

Palette — seven roles
#FCFCFAGround. Where the Arabic lives.
#F1F0ECRail. The apparatus column and all chrome.
#D8D7D1Rule. Hairlines and cell edges.
#111211Ink. Arabic and headings. 18.3:1 on ground.
#5C5F5CApparatus ink. Rail body and labels. 6.3:1 on ground, 5.7:1 on rail tint.
#2437C4Index blue. The only accent: links, focus, anchor.

One hue, one job. A system with a single accent cannot build a good/bad scale: there is nothing to pair the blue against. Blue means "you are here" and nothing else. Grade rank is a hanging bracketed count in the rail — never a colour, never a chip. A green was tried in this slot and cut, on two grounds: a system whose thesis is one accent loses that thesis the moment a second hue can mean status, and in this subject green is the colour of Paradise and of the Prophet’s garments — weight no rail label should carry. Blue, by contrast, reads in Islamic art as heaven, protection and knowledge, is the link colour of the Arabic web, and is the one hue that survives colour-vision deficiency. Never green, never gold, never red.
