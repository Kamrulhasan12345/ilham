Apparatus — one system, two grounds

The Arabic is the object on the table, set at 44px. The English interface shrinks to a quiet apparatus around it: labels at 12px, one accent, no serifs anywhere.

Ground

Foundations

Seven roles, two grounds

One hue, one job. A system with a single accent cannot build a good/bad scale, because there is nothing to pair the blue against. Index blue means "you are here" and nothing else. Grade rank is a word in the rail and a bracketed weight — never a colour, never a chip.

Never green, never gold, never red. A green was tried in the accent slot and cut. A system whose thesis is one accent loses that thesis the moment a second hue can mean status — and in this subject green carries direct religious weight. Charcoal and green together is a banner before it is an interface.

Each swatch shows light on the left and dark on the right. The ratios are against that ground.

Ground#FCFCFA / #16181AThe page. Charcoal on dark, never #000 — pure black maximises halation.
Rail#F1F0EC / #1E2124The apparatus column and all chrome. On dark it is the lighter surface — the only elevation cue the system has.
Rule#D8D7D1 / #2E3236Decorative hairline, 1.4:1. Grouping is carried by the 48px gutter and the rail tint; a separator is exempt from 1.4.11, a control boundary is not — that is Edge.
Ink#111211 / #E4E2DCArabic and headings. 18.3:1 light · 12.5:1 dark on the panel surface. A softened white, never #FFF.
Apparatus ink#5C5F5C / #B4B8B3Every English label and rail word. 6.3:1 light · 8.0:1 dark, measured on the panel surface so the AAA floor holds where the text really sits.
Index blue#2437C4 / #93A8F5The only accent: links, focus, anchor, the link that sets a score. 7.1:1 on the dark panel — roughly 20 points less saturation than light.
Machine ink#3B3E42 / #A9AEB4Database values. Always quieter than the ink, on both grounds.

Contrast floors: light 4.5:1 body and 3:1 for UI and large text; dark 7:1 body, because the AAA threshold is a real readability gain once halation is in play. Shipped values clear both.

The Arabic ladder overlaps the Latin one only at the bottom. Body Arabic at 44px sits against body English at 16px — a 2.75× ratio between the two scripts, which is what vocalised Scheherazade needs to feel the same weight as a 16px grotesque. Arabic never inherits a Latin size.

Only Regular has a truly drawn Bold in Scheherazade New, so anything heavier risks a face the app synthesised. Emphasis on Arabic is therefore a 2px underline in Index blue, never a weight.

What the dark ground changes, and why. Body English 16 → 17px with +0.01em tracking, the standard optical correction for small text on dark. Arabic line-height 1.9 → 1.95, because vowel marks bloom and the extra 0.05 buys the clearance back without touching the size. Arabic stays Regular — the argument that halation thins light strokes applies at body size, and this Arabic is at 44px, where bloom dominates instead. Nothing else in the ladder moves, so a screen can switch ground without reflowing.

Foundations

Rules, not boxes

SpaceA doubling scale on a 6px base — five steps only. Fewer steps means the rhythm still reads when a screen is full.

6 12 24 48 96

RadiusSquare everywhere, except interactive chips and filters, which are fully round. Roundness in this system means one thing: you can click it.

Square — a stated fact

BordersOne or two edges, never four. Grouping is the rail tint and 48px of air. No cards, no panels, no shadows.

A hairline under a row, a 2px rule beside a stated fact, and the rail tint. That is every grouping device the system has.

A 2px ink rule on the inline start edge marks a rule the reader must not miss.
The same rule in Index blue marks position — the thing you are currently on.

FocusA 2px outline at 2px offset on :focus-visible, with a forced-colors fallback. Never a box-shadow — it disappears in Windows High Contrast Mode.

A focused link

Targets24px minimum, 44px on a coarse pointer. No page scrolls sideways; wide content scrolls inside its own box.

Buttons and inputs are 44px tall. Chips are 28px and never the only way to reach a destination.

Vocabulary

Five marks, and not one of them is a grade

Rank is never a colour and never a chip. A mark on the spine reports one thing: whether this link resolved to a person. How good that person is comes from the grade words in the rail and the bracketed weight. This is the whole safety argument — with one accent there is nothing to pair the blue against, so the system cannot build a good/bad scale even by accident. If the screen works in greyscale, it works.

The schema keeps graded, named but ungraded and unnamed apart, and it splits "no link" into three different reasons. The tempting mistake is one good-to-bad ramp, which renders "we do not know" as "this is bad" — a claim the data never makes. Here the three grade states are three different sentences, and no ramp exists to put them on.

  • Filled disc · a person res A · B

    A profile matched. The link opens it. Whether a scholar graded them, and how, is a sentence in the rail — never this mark.

  • Hollow disc · ambiguous res C

    The name fits more than one narrator. We know the name, not the person, so it does not link. Weight 0.15.

  • Dashed hollow disc · unresolved res X

    No profile matched at all. The raw string stays on screen, and the loader never removes the row.

  • Dashed hollow square · placeholder

    The source itself records an unidentified narrator. A square, because this gap is a different kind from a name we could not place. Weight 0.15.

  • Filled square · the collector

    Always first on the page, always excluded from the computation, and never a failure.

The three grade states, as sentences. This is what replaced the colour ramp, and each one is written out in full next to the name it belongs to:

gradedtrustworthy and precise ثقة ثبت · wt 0.88
named, not gradedidentified, but no scholar graded him — neutral, not a fault · wt 0.50
unnamedthe source records no name here · wt 0.15

A reader who never learns what a filled disc means still reads every one of these, because they are words. That is the point of moving the state out of the mark: the mark is now a fact about the database, and the judgement is prose a beginner can read.

What Index blue marks in a chain. The one link that sets the score, and nothing else — a 2px underline under the name plus the words "sets the score". Never a ring, never a fill. Most of the corpus has its minimum at ثقة, and marking that row as a problem would tell the reader that the strongest chain in the collection is full of them.

Signature element

The fixed rail

A 210px rail runs down every screen in the product, at the same x position, holding whatever English the current object needs: labels, identifiers, grades, glosses, the teacher's note on a student. It never changes width and it never wraps around the Arabic. Learning the product means learning one rail — and because it is a fixed column, a teacher can point at it in class.

Foundations

Three durations, and one thing motion may never do

The animation literature contradicts itself, and the split runs through this product. Smooth transitions between views halved the error rate in controlled tests, and short transitions measured as effective as long ones. Animation of data has never outperformed a well-made static diagram. So Apparatus animates the viewpoint and refuses to animate a grade, a weight, a generation or a chain position. Full trail in the motion report.

The tokensNamed for the job, not the length. One easing curve — linear motion reads as unnatural, ease-out is what something arriving does.

--dur-micro: 120ms — hover, press, fill, a row leaving a filter. Anything that repeats dozens of times an hour lives here, because a delay met repeatedly becomes friction rather than polish.

--dur-move: 180ms — the route change and disclosures. A viewpoint change, which is the one case the evidence positively supports.

--dur-enter: 240ms — an element arriving for the first time. The ceiling: NN/g calls 400ms very slow and 500ms "a real drag".

--ease-out: cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1)

Scale before speedThe trigger for motion sensitivity is how far something moves, not how long it takes.

Nothing travels more than 8px. Nothing scales, parallaxes, loops, or moves in the periphery while someone is reading — peripheral movement forces an involuntary shift of attention, which on a page of hadith is an interruption dressed as a feature.

Reduced motionCollapse the duration, keep the state change.

Under prefers-reduced-motion every duration becomes 0.01ms — but the route still changes, the filtered row still disappears, the readout still updates. Motion is never the only carrier of a change here, which is the test for whether it was decorative. Nuking motion wholesale is its own failure: a transitional interface can help readers with cognitive disabilities follow a change.

The one exceptionDocumented, not hidden.

The three corpus figures on the hub count up over 520ms on first load, breaking both the 240ms ceiling and the no-animated-data rule. Permitted because it runs once per session, is an entrance rather than a response to input, and the final figure is the only one anyone reads.

Implementation · both grounds

Nine rules the research settled

These apply to Apparatus light and dark alike. They are not stylistic preferences.

  1. Bidi isolation

    Every Arabic string carries an explicit dir; database-sourced strings use dir="auto" or <bdi>. HTML markup, not the CSS property — it works without CSS, and the attribute isolates as well as directs. The rail's Latin transmission word inside an Arabic name is the case that breaks without it.

  2. No tracking on Arabic

    Letter-spacing is zero on every Arabic element, at every size. Arabic is a joined script, and tracking pulls the joins apart. The +0.12em in this system belongs to 12px Latin labels only.

  3. No italic, no synthetic bold

    Arabic is never slanted and never emphasised by weight — only Scheherazade New's Regular has a truly drawn Bold, so anything else risks a face the app synthesised. Emphasis on Arabic is a 2px underline in Index blue. Italics are for English glosses.

  4. Ragged, never justified

    Arabic is right-aligned and ragged everywhere. Inter-word justification produces rivers and uneven colour, letter-level adjustment carries Arabic-specific problems, and there is no hyphenation to fall back on.

  5. Numerals, on the record

    Machine values use Western digits, because they sit inside an English apparatus rather than inside Arabic prose. Stated as a decision, not a default: the i18n libraries treat Arabic-Indic digits as the set for Arabic-language text, and web products diverge from that widely.

  6. Contrast floors

    Light ground: 4.5:1 body. Dark ground: 7:1 body, because the AAA threshold is a real readability gain once halation is in play. Every figure is measured against the panel surface, not the ground — the ground flatters each one by about 10%. Shipped dark: ink 12.5:1, apparatus ink 8.0:1, accent 7.1:1. Control boundaries clear 3:1 via --edge; decorative hairlines are separators and exempt.

  7. Focus, outline only

    A 2px solid outline at 2px offset, on :focus-visible, with a forced-colors fallback. Never a box-shadow — it disappears in Windows High Contrast Mode. 2px is the published minimum, not the old 1px.

  8. Colour, fenced

    One accent, blue, meaning position only. No green, no gold, no red anywhere. Grade and weight are never coloured. Charcoal and green never appear together. If the screen works in greyscale, it works.

  9. QA, off the desk

    Two checks no contrast tool performs: read the dark ground in a dim room on an OLED panel at low brightness, and proof fully vowelled Arabic at final size, where stacked marks fail first. Also verify mark placement in the shipping font — kasra with shadda moves.

The system

Deliberately left out

  • Serif English. The serif voice is reserved for the Arabic, with no exceptions.
  • A second accent colour — and therefore any colour-coded status at all. Grade, weight, resolution, mastery, overdue: every one of them is a word.
  • Cards and panels. Only the rail tint and 48px of air group anything.
  • Mid-size type. Between 21px and 28px the system has nothing.
  • Pure black and pure white, at either end of the dark ground.
  • Heavier Arabic. Emphasis is an underline, never a weight.
  • Shadows and glows. On dark, elevation is the lighter rail surface and nothing else.
  • Auto-switching by time of day. The student chooses the ground and it persists.
  • A recursive chain walk. Isnad positions are explicit rows, so a chain walk is aggregation. Recursion here would be artificial.
  • A force-directed narrator graph as a primary view. The adjacency table is the primary view.

Still open, and it needs the teacher. The pairing test: build the chain viewer twice, the second on IBM Plex Sans with IBM Plex Sans Arabic, and choose in front of the class. The sources favour one drawn family; Apparatus mixes deliberately. That trade is not ours to settle.
